What the circle sizes mean. Causes of death are sized by the lifetime risk of dying from that cause from the chosen age, computed from approximate 2023 US death rates by age (both sexes). Risk factors start from published population attributable fractions, then are rescaled with age by the mix of downstream causes they feed: factors that act through overdose, traffic or homicide shrink, those acting through heart disease and dementia hold or grow. Relative risks that weaken with age are not modeled. Attributable shares overlap heavily (obesity raises blood pressure and blood sugar; education acts through smoking and income), so they don't add up to 100%.

What the arrows mean. Direction of causation, not strength. Double-headed arrows mark feedback loops that unfold over time (depression with sleep, activity and social connection). Age affects nearly every node and is left off. Effect modification and unmeasured confounders such as conscientiousness are not drawn.

Sources. Danaei et al., PLoS Med 2009; US Burden of Disease Collaborators, JAMA 2018; Galea et al., AJPH 2011; Goh, Pfeffer & Zenios 2016; Blair, BJSM 2009; Cuijpers et al. 2014; Cappuccio et al. 2010; CDC NCHS and ARDI; NHTSA. Estimates come from different years (2000–2023) and are approximate.
